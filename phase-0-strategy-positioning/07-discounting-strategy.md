# Discounting Strategy

---

## What It Is

The rules that govern when, how much, and to whom you offer price reductions. Without a written policy, discounting happens reactively — sales reps improvise, win-back emails stack offers, and customers learn to game the system. A policy eliminates ambiguity and protects both unit economics and perceived value.

The core tension: discounts drive short-term conversions but damage long-term unit economics. Industry data consistently shows discounting lowers customer LTV by ~30% and trains customers to expect lower pricing. Every discount decision should be weighed against this.

---

## The Golden Rule

Never lead with a discount. Discounts are a closing tool, not an opening one. Offering a discount before demonstrating value signals that you don't believe in your own product's worth. It trains prospects to wait for a better offer and extends deal cycles.

If discounting feels necessary frequently, the problem is positioning or sales process — not price.

---

## Annual Billing Discount

Always offer 20% off for annual billing (equivalent to ~2.4 months free). This is the industry-standard expectation. Show the monthly-equivalent price on the pricing page with the discount prominently labeled when the annual toggle is selected.

Annual billing improves retention by ~30% due to payment psychology — customers are reluctant to lose something they've already paid for. It also improves cash flow and reduces churn surface area.

Never offer less than 20% for annual — it won't feel meaningful. Never offer more — it erodes the value of the monthly plan.

## Launch Discounts

### Early Bird Pricing
Run a time-limited discount (20-30% off) for the first cohort of users during launch. Enforce a hard 3-7 day window. When it ends, it ends — letting it run longer trains the market that your deadlines are fake.

Frame early bird pricing as exclusive access for founding members, not as a clearance sale. The framing matters: "founding member" signals privilege; "discount" signals desperation.

### Founder / Lifetime Deals
Lifetime deals (one-time fee for permanent access) are a strategic tool for pre-revenue proof-of-concept and early community building — not a pricing strategy. Use them only if you have a specific reason: building a launch community, proving market demand, or raising a cash spike.

The cost is permanent margin loss. Every lifetime customer is a user you will never bill again. Before doing an LTD:
- Calculate the breakeven month (when would they have paid you equivalent MRR)
- Cap the number of LTD spots (100-500 max)
- Define a clear upgrade path: how does a lifetime user become a paying subscriber if they want more?

Platforms like AppSumo expand reach but attract deal-seekers, not ideal customers. Weigh the community benefit against the margin cost carefully.

### Launch-Week Coupon Codes
A single coupon code for launch week (e.g., LAUNCH25) is an effective Product Hunt / launch day tactic. Limit redemptions to 200-500. Set a hard expiry date. Do not extend it.

## Pricing Page

Show the annual billing discount prominently on the pricing page — this is the only evergreen discount that belongs there. No other discount should appear on the pricing page by default.

Gate all other discounts to high-intent moments: checkout abandonment, post-trial emails, win-back sequences. Do not surface coupon fields or discount messaging on top-of-funnel pages (blog, homepage). Showing discounts to low-intent traffic trains visitors to expect lower pricing before they've understood your value.

---

## Trial Conversion Email Drip

In a free trial drip sequence, introduce the discount offer at email 4 or 5 only — after the value case has been established. Emails 1-3 should build value through education, social proof, and use case examples.

**Recommended sequence:**
- Email 1 (Day 0): Welcome + quick start. No discount.
- Email 2 (Day 2-3): Key feature + customer outcome. No discount.
- Email 3 (Day 5): Objection handling or case study. No discount.
- Email 4 (Day 7-8): "Trial ending soon" reminder. First soft mention of offer if needed.
- Email 5 (Day 10-12): Final email with time-limited offer — 15-20% off first 3 months or first year.

The discount in email 4-5 should have a hard deadline tied to the trial end date. "Upgrade before your trial ends and get 20% off your first year" is concrete and urgent.

---

## Post-Trial (No Conversion)

If a user's trial ends without conversion, send a separate 3-email win-back sequence:
- Email 1 (Day 1 post-trial): Recap of what they built/used during trial. No discount.
- Email 2 (Day 3): A customer story relevant to their use case. Soft CTA.
- Email 3 (Day 5-7): Limited-time offer — 20% off first 3 months, expires in 72 hours.

If they don't convert after this, stop emailing for 60 days. Aggressive follow-up damages brand trust.

## Win-Back (Churned Customers)

Win-back campaigns recover 10-15% of churned customers at a fraction of new customer acquisition cost. The discount should be tailored to the stated churn reason — a discount doesn't fix a missing feature.

**Sequence:**
- Email 1 (Day 1 after cancellation): Empathetic acknowledgment. No discount.
- Email 2 (Day 4): Address the stated churn reason directly.
- Email 3 (Day 7-14): Win-back offer (see amounts below), with a direct resubscribe link.
- Stop after 3 emails. Do not pursue further.

**Amounts by churn reason:**
- Price-related: 30% off for 3 months (Stripe coupon, applied automatically on click)
- Missing features: Free access to a higher tier for 3 months
- Technical issues or bad experience: Offer onboarding call + regular plan (no discount needed)
- Switched to competitor: Feature comparison + 30% off for 3 months

**Limits:** Never offer the win-back discount to a user who has already received it once. If they cancel and come back and cancel again, send emails 1 and 2 only — no discount on the second churn. Repeat discounters will cancel-and-return just for the offer.

Do not send win-back emails to users who cancelled during their trial. They never paid — a discount makes no sense.

## B2B and Enterprise Discounting

Discounting in enterprise sales is expected in some contexts, but the framing and timing matter.

**Acceptable:**
- 10-20% off for 2-3 year commitments (above $25k ACV)
- 5-10% for strategic reference customers (confirmed, documented case study)
- Volume/seat discounts as a published tiered rate (not custom negotiation)
- Competitive displacement: 10-20% + migration support, only if they're genuinely switching

**Not acceptable:**
- Discounts offered early in the sales conversation
- Case-by-case pricing that creates two customers at wildly different rates for the same product
- Matching competitor prices on demand (signals no differentiation)
- Discounting authority given to reps as a default objection response

Anchor enterprise conversations on business outcomes first. Customer success teams that anchor renewals on measurable ROI see 18% higher renewal rates and 27% less discount pressure.

## Nonprofit and Student Discounts

Offer a discounted rate for verified nonprofits and students. Standard rates: nonprofits 50%, students/educators 40%.

Verify eligibility: .edu email for students; 501(c)(3) documentation or Charity Navigator listing for nonprofits. Do not offer self-reported discounts — require verification.

Keep these discounts on a separate, ungated page (e.g., /nonprofit). Do not advertise them on the main pricing page to avoid training regular customers to seek these rates.

## Discount Amounts — Safe Ranges

| Use Case | Safe Range | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Annual billing | 20% | Less than 20% (not motivating) |
| Launch / early bird | 20-30% | >40% (signals desperation) |
| Trial conversion offer | 15-20% | >25% before demonstrating value |
| Win-back | 25-30% for 3 months | Forever duration (Stripe: avoid) |
| Enterprise commitment | 10-20% | >20% without multi-year commitment |
| Nonprofit / student | 40-50% | >60% (creates pricing confusion) |

Discounts above 40% consistently correlate with lower LTV, higher churn, and customers who are anchored on the discounted price forever. If your economics require >40% discounts to convert, the problem is positioning, ICP fit, or pricing level — not discount size.

## Stripe Implementation

Use Stripe coupons (backend discount definition) and promotion codes (customer-facing codes) separately. Multiple promo codes can map to the same coupon — useful for tracking which channel drove redemptions.

**Duration rules:**
- "Once" — for single-period offers (one month free)
- "Repeating" with a set number of months — for win-back offers (30% off for 3 months)
- "Forever" — avoid. This creates permanent margin loss. If a discount needs to be permanent, lower the base price instead.

Set `expires_at` on every promotion code. Hard deadlines must be enforced — a "LAUNCH2026" code that still works in 2027 signals your brand doesn't honor its own commitments.

Use one promo code per channel to track performance: `EMAIL25`, `EXITPOP20`, `WINBACK30`. Review redemption rates quarterly and retire codes with poor ROI.

Stripe Checkout supports one coupon or promotion code per checkout session — you cannot stack multiple codes. If stacking is required for a specific use case, implement it in custom server-side logic, not via Checkout's default promo code field.

## What Damages Value

- **Perpetual discounts**: Train customers to never pay full price and to anchor on the lower amount.
- **Stacking discounts**: Coupon + volume + annual combined creates a pricing floor customers will always expect.
- **Offering discounts early**: Before value is established, a discount confirms the product isn't worth the ask.
- **Case-by-case negotiation**: Similar customers who discover they paid different rates become resentful. Publish your discount policy.
- **Extending deadlines**: If the "72-hour offer" becomes a "96-hour offer" because a customer asked, you've trained every future customer to ask.
- **Announcing discounts to unqualified traffic**: Discount mentions on blog posts or top-of-funnel pages reshape price expectations before a visitor has understood the product.

## What to Test

- Annual plan shows 20% discount on pricing page toggle
- Promotion codes apply correctly at Stripe Checkout
- Expired promotion codes are rejected at checkout with a clear error
- Win-back email 3 link auto-applies the coupon on click
- Win-back discount is not sent to a user who already received it once
- Win-back discount is not sent to users who cancelled during trial
- Redemption-capped promo codes stop working after the limit is reached

## Edge Cases

- **Customer asks for a discount not in policy** — "We don't offer custom discounts, but we do have annual pricing at 20% off" is the full answer. Do not negotiate outside the published policy.
- **Win-back customer cancels again** — Remove them from win-back discount eligibility in your database on first redemption. Send emails 1-2 only on second churn.
- **Promo code works on the wrong plan** — Set product restrictions in Stripe when creating the coupon. Test in sandbox before sending any campaign.
- **Customer applies annual coupon then downgrades to monthly** — The coupon should be scoped to the annual plan only. Verify Stripe product restrictions are set correctly.
- **LTD customer wants to upgrade** — Pre-define the upgrade path before the LTD sale (e.g., LTD unlocks Starter tier features; upgrading to Pro is billed at standard rate with LTD as the baseline). Not having this defined creates a support nightmare.

## Common Mistakes

- Offering a discount at the first sign of sales resistance instead of handling the objection
- Creating "forever" duration Stripe coupons without realising the LTV damage
- Running the same win-back offer twice to repeat churners
- Placing discount codes or offers on the homepage or blog sidebar
- Setting a deadline and then extending it when a customer asks
- Not tracking which promo code drove each redemption (all codes map to same coupon, no channel visibility)
- LTD with no upgrade path defined
