# Competitor Analysis

---

## What It Is

A structured review of existing solutions in your space — direct competitors, indirect competitors, and alternative approaches. The goal isn't to copy them, it's to find gaps and positioning angles.

## Process

### Step 1: Identify Competitors

**Direct competitors** — solve the same problem for the same audience:
- Search "[your category] software" / "[your category] tool"
- Check G2, Capterra, Product Hunt, AlternativeTo
- Search Reddit: "best [category] tool" / "alternative to [known competitor]"
- Ask ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity: "what are the best tools for [problem]?"

**Indirect competitors** — solve the same problem differently:
- Spreadsheet/manual workflows
- Built-in features of larger platforms (e.g., Notion has basic project management)
- In-house custom tools

**Aim for:** 3-5 direct competitors, 2-3 indirect alternatives.

### Step 2: Analyze Each Competitor

For each direct competitor, document:

```
**Name:**
**URL:**
**Pricing:** [Free tier? Starting price? Enterprise?]
**Target audience:** [Who are they built for?]
**Core features:** [Top 5-7 features]
**Strengths:** [What do users praise?]
**Weaknesses:** [What do users complain about?]
**Review sentiment:** [Check G2, Capterra, Reddit, Twitter]
**Tech stack:** [If visible — helps understand constraints]
**Positioning:** [How do they describe themselves?]
**Founded / Funding:** [Startup vs established?]
```

### Step 3: Internal Positioning Matrix

This is an internal analysis artifact, not a published page. Public-facing comparison pages and tables are built in Phase 6 (marketing site) and Phase 14 (content) using this analysis as input.

| Feature | You | Competitor A | Competitor B | Competitor C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feature 1 | Yes | Yes | No | Partial |
| Feature 2 | Yes | No | Yes | Yes |
| Pricing (starter) | $X | $Y | $Z | $W |
| Free tier | Yes/No | Yes/No | Yes/No | Yes/No |

Don't try to win every row. Look for the rows where you're the only "Yes" — that's your angle.

### Step 4: Find the Gaps

Look for patterns in negative reviews and complaints:

- **Pricing complaints** — "too expensive for what it does", "hidden fees", "forces annual"
- **Complexity complaints** — "takes weeks to set up", "need a consultant", "bloated"
- **Missing features** — "I wish it had X", "had to use another tool for Y"
- **Support complaints** — "took 3 days to respond", "only email support"
- **Performance complaints** — "slow", "crashes", "laggy"

Each complaint is a potential positioning angle for you.

### Step 5: Define Your Positioning Angle

Based on the gaps, choose ONE primary differentiation:

- **Simpler** — "X without the complexity" (Linear vs Jira)
- **Cheaper** — "X at 1/10th the price" (Plausible vs Google Analytics... sort of)
- **Faster** — "Set up X in 5 minutes, not 5 weeks"
- **More focused** — "X built specifically for [niche]"
- **Better DX** — "X that developers actually enjoy" (Resend vs SendGrid)
- **Privacy-first** — "X without selling your data"
- **AI-native** — "X powered by AI from the ground up"

You can have secondary differentiators, but lead with one.

## Where It Gets Used

| Output | Use |
|---|---|
| Internal positioning matrix | Input for Phase 6 pricing-page comparison table and Phase 14 comparison content |
| Competitor weaknesses | Marketing copy angles |
| Positioning angle | One-liner, hero copy, everywhere |
| Gap analysis | Feature roadmap prioritization |

## Deliverables

- [ ] 3-5 direct competitors identified and analyzed
- [ ] 2-3 indirect alternatives documented
- [ ] Internal positioning matrix created
- [ ] Top 3 competitor weaknesses / gaps identified
- [ ] Primary positioning angle chosen

## Common Mistakes

- Only looking at direct competitors (the spreadsheet is your biggest competitor)
- Feature-matching instead of finding gaps (you'll never out-feature an established player)
- Ignoring pricing as a feature (being cheaper or having a better free tier is a real differentiator)
- Analysis paralysis (spending weeks on this instead of building)
- Not revisiting (competitors change — check quarterly)
