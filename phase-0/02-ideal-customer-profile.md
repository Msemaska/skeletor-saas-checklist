# Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

---

## What It Is

A detailed description of the type of person (B2C) or company (B2B) who gets the most value from your product. Not who *could* use it — who *should* use it. Your ICP drives every decision: features, pricing, copy, channels, support.

## Process

### Step 1: Define the Basics

**If B2C:**
- Role / job title
- Industry / field
- Experience level (junior, mid, senior)
- Tech savviness (builds tools vs uses tools)
- Budget sensitivity (free-tier hunter vs pays-for-quality)
- Where they hang out online (Reddit, Twitter, Discord, LinkedIn, forums)

**If B2B:**
- Company size (solo, 2-10, 11-50, 51-200, 200+)
- Industry / vertical
- Revenue range
- Tech stack (relevant to your product)
- Decision maker role (founder, engineering manager, marketing lead)
- Buying process (self-serve vs sales-led vs committee)

### Step 2: Identify the Trigger

What event or frustration makes someone start looking for a solution like yours?

- "I just spent 3 hours doing X manually"
- "My current tool broke / raised prices / got acquired"
- "My team grew past the point where spreadsheets work"
- "My boss asked me to find a solution for Y"

The trigger tells you *when* to reach them and *what messaging* to lead with.

### Step 3: Map Their Current Solution

What are they doing today instead of using your product?

- **Manual process** (spreadsheets, email, pen and paper)
- **Competitor** (which one? what do they hate about it?)
- **Built in-house** (custom scripts, internal tools)
- **Nothing** (they're ignoring the problem)

Each alternative implies different messaging:
- Manual → emphasize speed/automation
- Competitor → emphasize what you do differently
- In-house → emphasize maintenance burden
- Nothing → emphasize cost of inaction

### Step 4: Define What "Success" Looks Like for Them

After 30 days of using your product, what's different?

- What can they do that they couldn't before?
- What do they no longer have to do?
- What metric improved? (time saved, revenue up, errors down)

### Step 5: Identify Deal-Breakers

What would make them immediately dismiss your product?

- Missing a specific feature?
- Price above a threshold?
- No integration with a tool they depend on?
- Requires too much setup / migration effort?
- No mobile support?

## ICP Template

```
**Name:** [Give them a name — "Startup Sarah", "Agency Alex"]
**Role:** [Job title / description]
**Company:** [Size, industry, stage]
**Trigger:** [What made them start looking]
**Current solution:** [What they use today]
**Pain points:** [Top 3 frustrations]
**Success metric:** [What "working" looks like]
**Budget:** [What they'd pay monthly]
**Channels:** [Where to reach them]
**Deal-breakers:** [What makes them leave]
```

## How Many ICPs?

- **Launch:** 1. Just one. Pick the person you understand best and can reach most easily.
- **Post-traction:** 2-3 max. Each ICP needs its own messaging, onboarding, and potentially pricing.

## Where It Gets Used

| Decision | How ICP Informs It |
|---|---|
| Feature prioritization | Would my ICP use this? |
| Pricing | What can my ICP afford? What feels fair to them? |
| Marketing channels | Where does my ICP spend time? |
| Copy and tone | How does my ICP talk about this problem? |
| Onboarding flow | What does my ICP need to see first? |
| Support level | Does my ICP expect self-serve or hand-holding? |

## Deliverables

- [ ] One primary ICP fully defined (using template above)
- [ ] Trigger event identified
- [ ] Current solution mapped
- [ ] Success metric defined
- [ ] Top 3 channels to reach them identified
- [ ] Deal-breakers listed

## Common Mistakes

- Too broad ("small business owners" — which ones?)
- Demographic-focused instead of behavior-focused (age/location don't matter, pain points do)
- Aspirational instead of realistic (targeting enterprise when you're a solo founder)
- Never talking to actual humans who match the profile
