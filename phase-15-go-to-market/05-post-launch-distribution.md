# Post-Launch Distribution

---

## What It Is

Ongoing distribution activities after the initial launch — getting listed in niche directories, guest posting on industry blogs, contributing to curated lists, and building backlinks over time. This is the long game that builds domain authority and sustained organic traffic.

## Niche Directories

Beyond the general startup directories submitted at launch (phase-15-go-to-market/02-launch-distribution.md), submit to directories specific to your industry or audience.

**Design decisions:**
- Identify 10 niche directories for your category by searching "[your category] directory", "[your industry] tools list", "best [category] for [audience]"
- Submit to each with the listing description from the copy bank — don't write custom copy per directory
- Prioritise directories that rank on page 1 for your category keywords — a listing on a page-1 directory is a backlink and a discovery channel
- Track which directories are submitted, pending review, and live in a Notion table

## Guest Posts

Writing articles for other blogs in your industry — the article provides value to their audience, and you get a backlink and brand exposure.

**Design decisions:**
- Target 2 guest posts per month
- Identify blogs by searching "[your category] blog", "[your industry] tips", and checking where your competitors have been featured
- Pitch topics that are relevant to the host blog's audience AND mention your product naturally — not a product review, a genuinely useful article that references your tool as one way to solve the problem
- The article links to your site from the author bio AND from a contextual mention within the article body
- Write the article yourself (Claude drafts, you edit) — don't ask the host to write about your product
- Guest posts also serve LLM optimization (phase-14-content/13-llm-optimization.md) — third-party mentions on authoritative sites increase AI citation likelihood

## GitHub Awesome Lists

Curated lists of tools, libraries, and resources on GitHub. Getting listed is a high-quality backlink and a discovery channel for developer audiences.

**Design decisions:**
- Search GitHub for "awesome-[your category]" lists
- Read the list's contribution guidelines before submitting a PR
- Your PR should include: product name, one-line description, and a link. Match the format of existing entries exactly.
- Only submit to lists where your product genuinely belongs — irrelevant submissions get rejected and damage your reputation
- Submit to 3 relevant awesome-lists

## Forum & Community Engagement

Ongoing helpful participation in communities where your audience hangs out.

**Design decisions:**
- Identify 3 communities: relevant subreddits, Discord servers, and Slack communities
- Participate genuinely — answer questions, share insights, help people. Not every post needs to mention your product.
- When someone asks a question your product solves, mention it as one option alongside other solutions. Don't make every comment about your product.
- Build reputation over time — community members who recognise you as helpful will check out your product on their own.
- This is ongoing, not a one-time task. 15 minutes per day of community engagement.

## Backlink Monitoring

Track which sites link to you and which links you've built.

**Design decisions:**
- Google Search Console shows which sites link to you (Links report)
- Review monthly — which new backlinks appeared, are any from unexpected sources
- Track all outreach (directory submissions, guest post pitches) in a Notion table with: site, status (submitted, pending, published, rejected), date, URL

## What to Test

- Niche directory submissions are live and linking to your site
- Guest post articles are published with author bio link and contextual product mention
- GitHub awesome-list PRs are accepted
- Community engagement is happening consistently (15 minutes per day)
- Google Search Console shows backlinks from submitted directories and guest posts

## Edge Cases

- **Directory charges for listing** — skip it unless it's a high-authority directory that your competitors are listed on. Free listings are sufficient for an MVP.
- **Guest post pitch rejected** — don't follow up more than once. Move to the next blog on your list. The pitch may not have been a fit for their audience.
- **Awesome-list PR rejected** — read the rejection reason. If your product doesn't meet their criteria, respect that. Don't re-submit.
- **Community calls you out for self-promotion** — if someone says you're promoting too much, they're right. Pull back to purely helpful engagement for 2 weeks before mentioning your product again.

## Implementation Checklist

- [ ] 10 niche directories identified and submitted
- [ ] Directory submission tracking in Notion (site, status, date, URL)
- [ ] 2 guest post targets identified per month
- [ ] Guest post pitch template created
- [ ] 3 GitHub awesome-lists identified and PRs submitted
- [ ] 3 communities identified for ongoing engagement
- [ ] 15 minutes per day community engagement habit established
- [ ] Google Search Console backlink report reviewed monthly
- [ ] All outreach tracked in Notion

## Common Mistakes

- Submitting to every directory without checking if it's relevant or has authority
- Guest post pitches that are thinly disguised product reviews
- Spamming communities with product links
- Not tracking outreach (duplicate submissions, missed follow-ups)
- Stopping after launch (distribution is ongoing, not a one-time event)
- Paying for backlinks (violates Google guidelines, risk of penalty)
- Ignoring awesome-lists (high-quality backlinks with targeted audience)
