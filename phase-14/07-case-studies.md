# Case Studies

---

## What It Is

Stories of real customers using your product to achieve a specific outcome. They only become possible after you have paying customers.

## Structure

Every case study follows the same structure:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Headline | "[Customer] achieved [specific result] with [Your Product]" |
| Summary | 3 sentences: who, what problem, what result. The reader decides whether to continue based on this. |
| Customer Profile | Company name, industry, size, role of the person interviewed |
| The Problem | What they were doing before, why it wasn't working, the cost of the problem |
| The Solution | How they use your product specifically, which features matter most to them |
| The Results | Quantified outcomes with specific numbers (time saved, revenue gained, efficiency improved) |
| Quote | Direct quote from the customer — their words carry more weight than yours |
| CTA | "Get similar results — try [Your Product]" |

## Content Rules

**Design decisions:**
- Quantified results are mandatory. "They love it" is not a case study. "Reduced invoice processing time from 3 hours to 20 minutes" is a case study. If the customer can't provide numbers, help them calculate the impact.
- Use the customer's words, not yours. Record the conversation (with permission) and pull exact quotes.
- Focus on the transformation, not your features. The story is: problem → struggle → discovery → implementation → results. Your product is the tool, not the hero.
- Name the customer. Use their full company name and logo with permission ("Acme Corp, a 200-person logistics company"), not anonymous labels ("A Fortune 500 company").
- One case study per customer segment. If you serve agencies and freelancers, create one case study for each.
- Keep it under 800 words.

## How to Get Case Studies

**Design decisions:**
- Ask customers who have been active for 3 months and have measurable results
- Frame the ask as: "We'd love to feature your success story on our site — it's great exposure for your company too"
- Offer to write it for them — send 5 questions, they answer in 15 minutes, you write the case study and send for approval
- Send the 5 questions by email, not a call. Lower friction, better thoughtful answers.

**The 5 Questions:**
1. What were you using before, and what was the biggest frustration?
2. What made you try [Your Product]?
3. How do you use [Your Product] day-to-day? Which features matter most?
4. What specific results have you seen? (time saved, revenue impact, efficiency gain)
5. What would you tell someone considering [Your Product]?

## Where Case Studies Live

**Design decisions:**
- Each case study is an MDX page at /blog/case-study-customer-name (lives on the blog, tagged as "case-study")
- A dedicated /case-studies index page lists all case studies with: customer logo, headline, one-line result summary
- Case studies are also embedded on the landing page (social proof section) as short cards: customer logo + one-line result + link to full case study
- Distribute via social media (thread format: problem → solution → result → CTA) and newsletter

## Production Pipeline

1. Identify happy customers with measurable results (3+ months active)
2. Send the 5 questions via email
3. Claude drafts the case study from their answers
4. Human edit: verify quotes are accurate, add product screenshots showing their use case, format results as bold callout numbers
5. Send to customer for approval before publishing
6. Publish, distribute, add to landing page social proof section

## What to Test

- Case study follows the standard structure (headline, summary, profile, problem, solution, results, quote, CTA)
- Results include specific numbers
- Customer name and logo are used (with permission)
- Direct customer quote is included
- Case study is under 800 words
- Case study index page lists all case studies
- Landing page social proof section includes case study cards

## Edge Cases

- **Customer doesn't want to be named** — use their first name and company type ("Sarah, founder of a 50-person marketing agency") instead of full company name. Still better than fully anonymous.
- **Customer can't quantify results** — help them calculate. "How many hours did you spend on [task] before? How many now?" Turn qualitative feedback into numbers.
- **Customer churns after publishing** — keep the case study live. The results were real at the time. Only remove if the customer requests it.
- **Customer's product/company changes** — update the customer profile section if they rebrand or grow significantly. The results and story remain the same.

## Common Mistakes

- No quantified results ("they loved it" is not evidence)
- Anonymous case studies (no name, no logo)
- Writing in your voice instead of the customer's (use their quotes)
- Case study that reads like a product brochure instead of a customer story
- Over 1500 words
- Only one case study for all audiences
- Not asking for case studies
- Waiting until you have 100 customers to start (get the first one after your first 5 happy users)
